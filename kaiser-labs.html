Understanding the AST/ALT Ratio

The AST/ALT ratio (also known as the De Ritis ratio) helps clinical providers evaluate liver health.

< 1.0 Typical in Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD/MASLD): ALT levels are usually higher than AST in early-stage metabolic liver disease.
> 1.0 Sign of Disease Progression: A ratio above 1.0 in someone with fatty liver may suggest progression to advanced scarring (fibrosis or cirrhosis).
≈ 2.0 or higher Indicative of Alcoholic Liver Injury: Ratios close to or exceeding 2.0 strongly point to alcohol-related liver injury or hepatitis.

Note: A slightly elevated ratio is common and benign if your absolute AST and ALT levels are completely within their normal reference ranges. Ratios should always be interpreted by a healthcare professional alongside your complete metabolic profile.

Understanding the TG/HDL Ratio

The Triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (TG/HDL) is an excellent marker of metabolic health, insulin sensitivity, and cardiovascular risk.

< 2.0 Ideal / Normal: Indicates good insulin sensitivity and a lower risk of cardiovascular disease.
2.0 - 3.0 Borderline / Moderate: Suggests mild metabolic dysfunction or suboptimal lipid balance.
> 3.0 High Risk / Insulin Resistance: Strongly correlated with insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and elevated risk of heart disease.

Note: A lower TG/HDL ratio is generally better. Optimal cardiovascular and metabolic targets may vary depending on demographic factors. Always consult your physician to interpret this ratio in the context of your overall health profile.
